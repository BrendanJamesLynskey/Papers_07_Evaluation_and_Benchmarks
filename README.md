# Papers 07 — Evaluation & Benchmarks

The seventh presentation in the Key LLM Publications series tackles the question that underlies every model decision: how do we actually know it works? It indexes five publications that form a ladder of evaluation. **MMLU** (Hendrycks et al., 2020 — arXiv:2009.03300) is the broad 57-subject multiple-choice knowledge test, evaluated zero/few-shot and now partly saturated. **HumanEval** (Chen et al., OpenAI, 2021 — arXiv:2107.03374) grades 164 Python problems by unit tests and introduced the **pass@k** functional-correctness metric. **BIG-bench** (Srivastava et al., 2022 — arXiv:2206.04615) is a 200+-task community benchmark built to be hard, spawning BIG-Bench Hard (BBH) where chain-of-thought helps most. **HELM** (Liang et al., Stanford CRFM, 2022 — arXiv:2211.09110) reframes evaluation as a transparent scenarios × metrics matrix, measuring accuracy, calibration, robustness, fairness, bias, toxicity and efficiency at once. And **Judging LLM-as-a-Judge** (Zheng et al., LMSYS, 2023 — arXiv:2306.05685) shows a strong LLM can grade with high human agreement, documents its position/verbosity/self-enhancement biases, and introduces MT-Bench and the crowdsourced Elo leaderboard, Chatbot Arena. Each slide gives the problem, the contribution, why it matters to a practising engineer, an original inline diagram, and a key takeaway.
